# Segregation at source - basics
Source: Solid Waste Management Rules, 2016 (MoEFCC, Government of India) - summary.

Every waste generator must segregate waste at source into three streams: biodegradable (wet) waste, non-biodegradable (dry) waste, and domestic hazardous waste, and hand it over to authorised waste collectors.
Wet waste includes kitchen waste, food leftovers, vegetable and fruit peels, tea leaves, coffee grounds, eggshells, flowers, garden leaves and food-soiled paper. Wet waste can be composted at home or handed over separately.
Dry waste includes clean plastic, paper, cardboard, metal, glass, rubber, thermocol, textiles and wood. Dry waste should be kept clean and dry so it can be recycled.
Domestic hazardous waste includes used batteries, discarded paint drums, pesticide cans, CFL and tube lights, expired medicines, broken mercury thermometers, used needles and syringes, and contaminated gauze.
Sanitary waste such as used diapers, sanitary pads and tampons must be securely wrapped in the pouches provided by manufacturers or in newspaper and placed in the dry waste bin or handed over separately as advised by the local body.
Waste must never be burnt, buried or thrown on streets, open spaces, drains or water bodies.
Commonly used bin colours in Indian cities: green bin for wet waste and blue bin for dry waste. Domestic hazardous waste is kept separately (often in a red or black bag or container). Colours can vary by city, so follow local municipal instructions.
