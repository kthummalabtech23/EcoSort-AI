# E-waste and batteries
Source: E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022 - summary.

E-waste includes mobile phones, chargers, cables, earphones, computers, keyboards, remote controls, small appliances, LED bulbs and electronic toys.
E-waste must not be put in household dry or wet waste. Hand it over to authorised e-waste collection centres, producer take-back programmes, or registered recyclers and dismantlers.
Used batteries (AA, AAA, button cells, mobile and laptop batteries) are domestic hazardous waste. Keep them separate, tape the terminals of lithium batteries, and hand them over to battery collection points or e-waste collectors.
Lithium-ion batteries can catch fire if crushed or punctured in garbage trucks. Never throw swollen or damaged batteries in the bin.
Remove batteries from devices before handing over electronics where possible, and erase personal data from phones and computers.
